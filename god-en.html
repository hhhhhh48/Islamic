Where did you come from?

من أين جئت؟

Pause for a moment, and answer honestly: You are reading these words right now. You have eyes that see, a mind that thinks, a heart that feels. Who gave you all of this?

Did you come from nothing? Did you create yourself? Or is there Someone who brought you into existence?

Just one question

If you found a phone on the ground, would you say "it formed itself"?
Then how can you say about yourself — far more complex than a billion phones — that you came to be without a Creator?

This is the cosmological argument — the simplest and strongest: everything that begins to exist requires a cause. You were not, then you were. And everything that was not and then was, must have a Maker who made it.

2

The phone in the desert

الهاتف في الصحراء

Imagine: you're walking in a barren desert. No humans, no footprints. Suddenly you find a smartphone on the sand — camera, buttons, a working screen.

Could you say: "It formed itself over millions of years"? Even the most stubborn atheist would refuse this. Why? Because a phone requires an intelligent maker.

The phone is made of: metals + plastic + glass. You are made of: cells + DNA + consciousness. The phone is billions of times less complex than you. So if the phone must have a maker, you have far more right to have a Maker — an infinitely wise Creator.

This is not an emotional conclusion — it's a self-evident rational judgment: every made thing has a maker, and every created thing has a Creator.

3

Astonishing cosmic precision

دقة الكون المذهلة

These are not opinions — they are verified physical facts acknowledged by astronomers of every belief:

  • Gravity: If it were stronger by 1 in 10120, the universe would have collapsed instantly. If weaker, matter would have scattered and no stars would form.
  • Distance Earth–Sun: Exactly 149.6 million km. 5% closer — we burn. 5% farther — we freeze.
  • Oxygen in air: Precisely 21%. At 25%, every forest would burn on the first lightning strike. At 15%, every animal would suffocate.
  • Strong nuclear force: If changed by 0.5%, no atom would exist — and no anything.
  • Cosmological constant: Tuned to 1 in 10120. A hair's difference, no universe.

Is all this coincidence?

If you took 100 letters in a bag, shook it, and a complete poem came out — who would believe you? So how about a whole universe of such precision?

4

The proof within you

الدليل من داخلك

Reflect on these truths about yourself — you don't need a laboratory, only quiet reflection:

  • Consciousness: No one knows how thoughts arise from brain cells. Thought is not matter, isn't measured, isn't seen. Who gave it to you?
  • Conscience: Even an atheist knows inside that killing innocents is evil. Where did this moral sense come from? Blind matter cannot be a source of morality.
  • Love: A mother shielding her child from fire with her body — does something chemistry and hormones cannot explain.
  • Yearning for the eternal: Every human, even an atheist, rejects the idea that his existence is meaningless. Who planted this yearning in you?

These are the proofs of the fitrah — the inner nature. Every soul carries the seed of belief in a Creator. The atheist does not deny it, but tries to bury it.

5

Why ONE God — not three, not a million?

لماذا إله واحد؟

Why not three gods? Or 33 million as in Hinduism? The logical answer is very simple:

  • If there were two gods, what if they disagreed? One wants something, the other opposes — who wins?
  • If one always overrides the other, the second isn't really a god.
  • If they're equal, the universe wouldn't be stable for a moment — but it has been stable for 13.8 billion years.
  • Therefore: there must be ONE God, all-powerful, undefeated, uncontested.
"Had there been within the heavens and earth gods besides Allah, they both would have been ruined."— Quran 21:22

As for "33 million gods" in Hinduism, or the "many gods" of Greek mythology — they are reflections of human minds who lived in societies with many tribal chiefs, and transferred that plurality to the sky.

6

"Who created God?" — A logically flawed question

"من خلق الله؟"

This question sounds clever, but it measures the Creator by the standards of the created. It's an outright logical error.

The architect sets the laws of building, but is himself not subject to those laws. God is the Creator of time and space, and He is beyond time and space — so it makes no sense to ask "who created Him?" because He is not created.

The decisive logical rule:

  • Everything with a beginning has a cause. But God has no beginning — He is the First before whom there is nothing.
  • If we said "God was created by something else," we'd fall into infinite regress — impossible.
  • The regress must end at the Necessary Being — whose existence is from Himself, not from another. That is God.

"Why doesn't He show Himself?"

"لماذا لا نراه؟"

We don't see many things that exist:

⚡ Electricity 🧲 Magnetism 🌬️ Wind 🧠 Mind 💭 Thoughts 🌌 Dark matter

All of these exist, yet we don't see them. But we see their effects and feel them. God's existence is perceived through His magnificent effects in the universe, not through physical vision.

The sun — come close and you burn; but you see its light and warmth from afar. Likewise God: eyes cannot grasp Him in this world, but you see traces of His power everywhere.

Moreover, seeing Him in this world is impossible — He is unlike anything, and you are limited, while God is unlimited. In the Hereafter, He has promised the believers the vision of Him — the greatest bliss of Paradise.

8

"Why does evil exist?"

"لماذا الشر؟"

A fair question, with a logical answer:

If God had created humans forced to do good, they'd be like angels — no freedom, no choice, no test. Faith would be worthless. Patience would mean nothing. Justice would have no place.

Freedom's price is the test. God gave you free will, made this world a test, and the Hereafter a place of reward. Whoever oppresses will face his outcome. Whoever is patient will receive his reward.

"And We did not wrong them, but they were the wrongdoers."— Quran 43:76
A surgeon may cut a patient to heal him — an apparent "harm," but really mercy. Many "evils" in your life you later understand were good for you.
